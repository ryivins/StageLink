Reflection

My thoughts, decisions, and learning experience while building StageLink.

Decisions

One of the biggest decisions I made during this project was choosing to build a visually focused website instead of a more text-heavy or technical one. Since StageLink is centered around live events, production, and stage design, I wanted the site to immediately communicate energy and professionalism.

Another important decision was keeping the navigation simple and predictable. I avoided overly complicated menus or interactions so users could move through the site easily.

I also focused heavily on responsive design early, since many users in this industry browse on mobile devices.

Finally, I kept the scope realistic to ensure the final product was polished rather than incomplete.

What Worked

The visual design and branding consistency worked well and helped the site feel like a real product.

The responsive layout improved significantly after using flexbox and better structure planning.

I also improved my understanding of layout hierarchy and page structure during this project.

Experimenting with CSS and iterating helped me become more confident in problem solving.

What I Would Do Differently

I would plan the layout more carefully before coding, using wireframes to avoid redesigning later.

I would also organize my CSS better from the beginning to improve maintainability.

Accessibility would be a bigger focus, especially semantic HTML and contrast checking.

I would add more interactivity such as animations or dynamic elements to improve engagement.

What I Learned

I learned that web development is about creating user experience, not just functionality.

I improved my understanding of flexbox, responsive design, and layout systems.

I learned that debugging is a structured process that requires patience and testing.

I also learned to balance perfection with progress instead of over-editing early stages.

Most importantly, I learned that web development is iterative and improves through refinement.

Reflection

Decisions

One of the biggest decisions I made during this project was choosing to build a visually focused website instead of a more text-heavy or technical one. Since StageLink is centered around live events, production, and stage design, I wanted the site to immediately communicate energy and professionalism through visuals, layout, and motion. I decided to prioritize strong sections like services, galleries, testimonials, and event categories because those are the areas users would most likely care about first.

Another important decision was keeping the navigation simple and predictable. I avoided overly complicated menus or interactions because I wanted visitors to move through the site naturally without confusion. I also chose a modern dark-themed aesthetic with strong contrast and large imagery because it fit the entertainment and production industry better than a minimalist corporate style would have.

I also made the decision to focus heavily on responsive design early instead of treating mobile support as an afterthought. A lot of event-related browsing happens on phones, so I spent time making sure sections stacked properly, text stayed readable, and images scaled correctly across screen sizes.

Finally, I chose to keep the scope realistic. There were ideas I considered adding, like advanced booking systems or backend functionality, but I realized that trying to build everything at once would reduce the overall quality of the project. Instead, I focused on polishing the front-end experience.

What Worked

The visual design worked better than I expected. The strongest part of the project is the overall presentation and branding consistency. The combination of sections, colors, spacing, and imagery helped the site feel like a real business website.

The responsiveness also turned out well. After reorganizing layouts with flexbox and responsive sizing, the site became much cleaner across devices.

I also became more comfortable with structuring pages and thinking about hierarchy and flow instead of placing elements randomly.

Another success was learning through experimentation. Testing different CSS approaches helped me become more independent as a developer.

What I Would Do Differently

If I restarted the project, I would spend more time planning before writing code. Wireframes or a clearer structure would have saved time.

I would also organize my code better from the beginning, especially CSS, to avoid repetition and improve maintainability.

Another improvement would be accessibility, including better semantic structure and contrast checking.

I would also add more interactivity, such as animations or dynamic content, to improve engagement.

What I Learned

This project taught me that web development is about creating a clear user experience, not just making something functional.

I learned a lot about flexbox and responsive design, and how layouts behave across different screen sizes.

I also learned that debugging is a major part of development and requires patience and structured thinking.

On a personal level, I learned that I tend to over-focus on perfection early, and I improved at balancing quality with progress.

Most importantly, I learned that web development is iterative, and improvement comes through constant refinement.
